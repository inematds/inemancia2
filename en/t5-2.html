Section 1 of 3·VIRALST sales

1.VIRALST in sales mode

The same four-beat structure works for selling too—but with a tonal adjustment. Instead of prompting reflection, the sales mode prompts desire, urgency, and action. The hook stops being just curiosity and becomes a hook built on pain or immediate desire. Identification becomes commercial empathy: “this solves my problem.” The conflict shows the obstacle and the discovery of the solution. And the closing adds a call to action to the insight.4

Each adjustment has a simple neuroscientific rationale: the pain hook activates the amygdala (attention and urgency); practical empathy recruits mirror neurons; the obstacle followed by the solution triggers dopamine through the pursuit of reward; and the insight with a CTA reinforces the decision. The secret is to keep emotion ahead of logic — short sentences, conversational rhythm, no jargon.

The prompt below puts this adaptation into practice. It’s the template that generates a script ready to record, with a title and hook variations:

[VIRALST SALES MODE ON] You’re an expert in viral storytelling and emotional persuasion. Create a short script (30 to 60 seconds) ready to record (Reels, TikTok, or Shorts), using the VIRALST technique adapted for SALES, with 4 elements: 1. HOOK (first 3 to 5s) - Start with an immediate dilemma, pain point, or desire of the audience. - Use provocative questions, contrasts between loss and gain, or bold promises. - Create urgency and curiosity ("this could change my results"). 2. IDENTIFICATION - Show empathy for the audience’s pain or current situation. - Use real-life situations and natural language. - Make viewers think, "that’s me" or "that’s exactly what I’m going through." 3. CONFLICT - Show the current mistake, waste, or limitation. - Present the discovery or turning point—when the solution appears. - Create contrast between before (frustration) and after (transformation). 4. CLOSING - Deliver the turnaround and the promised result. - Include a subtle, emotional call to action ("discover", "try it now", "follow the link"). - End with a sentence that sparks the desire to act. STYLE: human, persuasive language; short sentences with a spoken rhythm; emotion over logic; line breaks indicate pauses. OUTPUT: 1) complete script; 2) short title (max. 6 words); 3) three sales-focused HOOK variations. THEME: [product, service, or transformation to be sold]

O closing. In reflective mode, it delivers only the insight; in sales mode, it adds the call to action — an emotional, low-friction invitation (“try it now,” “click the link”) that turns understanding into a decision.

Section 2 of 3·Triggers (FGMD)

2.FGMD: the ten digital triggers

The four beats of a viral story unfold in digital spaces into a finer-grained map—the FGMD. The difference in organization is crucial: the triggers here are ordered by function in the brain, through marketing jargon. Each one answers a mental question the viewer asks, in sequence, from the first second through the final action.

The order is: attention (stop — “what is this?”), retention (hold on — “where is this going?”), understanding (clarify — “am I understanding?”), identification (connect — “is this about me?”), engagement (activate — “what do I think about this?”), authority (establish credibility — “why should I listen to you?”), awareness (awaken — “I never thought of it that way”), belonging (retain over the long term — “where do I fit in?”), transformation (show the way — “can I get there?”) and, only then, CTA (move — “what do I do now?”).5

The most overlooked trigger — and what sustains authority

Understanding is often skipped, and that's a costly mistake: if someone feels stupid, they leave—even if they're interested. Simplify, use metaphors, translate the jargon, offer a concrete example—that's what builds real authority. And notice where the CTA appears: last, after raising awareness and showing a path. A strong CTA doesn't ask; it continues the process. The summary sentence captures the whole logic: strong content doesn't take you from click to sale—it takes you from interruption to awareness.

▸ Going deeper: the master prompt for applying the ten triggers optional

Optional layer. FGMD becomes a single prompt that takes a topic and produces content applying the ten triggers in cognitive order, with a firm rule: no hype, no unrealistic promises, no technical terms without explanation. The content should feel like awareness, not advertising.

# Master Prompt — Complete Trigger Application You’re a digital communications strategist specializing in deep-seated mental triggers. I’ll give you ONE TOPIC. Create content that applies ALL the following triggers in an integrated, progressive way, respecting the brain’s cognitive order: 1. Attention — open with a disruption or a betrayal of expectations that stops the scroll. 2. Retention — an open-ended promise and cognitive tension to keep viewers watching. 3. Comprehension — translate into plain language, with a metaphor or example. 4. Identification — make the person recognize themselves in the problem. 5. Engagement — trigger a mental reaction or an implied question. 6. Authority — demonstrate command of the process without self-promotion. 7. Awareness — create a shift in perception (something you can’t “unsee”). 8. Belonging — make it clear that there is an ongoing space or path. 9. Transformation — present a possible and accessible path. 10. CTA — point to a clear, low-friction next step. # Rules: no hype, no unrealistic promises, no untranslated jargon. # Output: strong title; short blocks; relevant final CTA. Topic: [PASTE THE TOPIC HERE]

There’s also a short, one-line version, useful for a quick request: “create content about [topic] applying the ten triggers in order, in human language, without hype, and focused on awakening awareness.” The reference model is published at inematds.github.io/FGMD.

Section 3 of 3·NCIAFlux

3.Case study: the NCIAFlux project

All the persuasion in this learning path needs something to persuade in favor. NCIAFlux works as a case study because it shows how a promise becomes clear enough to fit in one sentence. It's a system of NeuroFluxo for people with ADHD — the name combines NCIA (from neuroscience) with Flux (flow state, adaptation, movement). The concept explained to the user is simple: the brain's flow in everyday life.6

The promise fits in a single line because it turns expectations on their head: “with NCIAFlux, you don't adapt to the method—the method adapts to your NeuroFlux.” Instead of demanding discipline, the system understands how a person's brain works, creates a workable plan, and adjusts the flow as their energy and routine change. It's a promise that doesn't sound clinical or productivity-obsessed, has a scientific basis—and is easy to explain in one sentence.

Three phases, and a differentiator that's almost entirely emotional

The product is organized into three phases. The Discovery (five to seven minutes) asks simple questions and provides immediate feedback, generating an initial profile — and ongoing use is optional. The Plan stems from this profile: one priority per day, a few light tasks, conditional routines. The Follow-up and gentle— a visual dashboard, optional text or voice chat, notifications at the person’s own pace. The differentiator, however, is defined almost entirely by what it doesn’t do: it doesn’t charge, compare, or punish. Notice how this echoes the persuasion in Section 1—the entire product speaks to the user’s fears and frustrations, not its own features.

Stop and predict

Above all reduce fears e justify failures: people with ADHD carry the guilt of abandoned planners and unmet goals. Saying “you don't adapt to the method” lifts that burden — the method was rigid and failed, not the person.

▸ Going deeper: why a short brand name + explained concept works optional

Optional layer. The project deliberately separates the brand of the concept: NCIAFlux is the short, strong name that sticks in your memory; NeuroFluxo is the one-sentence explanation anyone can understand. This pairing solves a common dilemma — descriptive names are clear but long; short names are memorable but opaque. Using both in layers gives you the best of both.

The project's code and development are open in the repository: github.com/inematds/NCIAFlux. It's worth completing the learning path here as an exercise: take NCIAFlux and write a VIRALST sales-mode script yourself —using the five triggers from Section 1 and the FGMD order from Section 4.
